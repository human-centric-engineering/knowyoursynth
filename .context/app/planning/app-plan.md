The plan for turning the Know Your Synth prototype into a live app that people sign in to
and use. The prototype is a single-page Claude artefact. The app has every synth in it,
user accounts and full interactivity, and is a companion to BeatBreaker. Written
2026-10-04 against `main` (`e1947370`). Nothing is built yet.

- **The brief** is [`prototype/BRIEF.md`](../../../prototype/BRIEF.md).
- **Its questions** (§8 of the brief) are answered in §10 here, as D1–D13.
- **The model** for this document is BeatBreaker's `.context/app/planning/app-plan.md`.

**This plan is written to load into the HCE Hub.** §6 has one block per phase and one per
feature, and each field is named for the Hub verb argument it becomes. §7 says how to load
it. Once it is loaded, the Hub is the record of what is planned, owned and done. This

## 1. Where the project is today

### The prototype

The prototype lives in `prototype/` (see its `README.md`, `BRIEF.md` and `CONTRACT.md`). It
is outside lint, format and type-check, so `npm run validate` does not judge it. What it
contains:

- **50 app files**, about 15,500 lines: engine, library, panel and UI.
- **106 synth data files**, about 46,800 lines.
- **25 synths** in total, with 2,938 controls, 1,226 jacks and 1,061 sounds with lessons.
- **8,931 decor items** on the panels.
- **A databank** of 184 artists, 248 songs, 86 instruments and 50 makers.

**Corrections to the brief**, checked by bundling `src/synths` with esbuild:

1. **Sounds are the bulk of the data.** They are 63% of the serialised size. Controls are
   17%, decor 9% and jacks 4%.
2. **Sounds contain no functions.** Every function is in the definitions: `toEngine`, `fmt`,
   and the jack functions `check`, `hear`, `amt` and `gain`.
3. **`toEngine` is short.** It runs to only 14–108 lines per synth. The panels themselves are
   generated by helper loops in code.
4. **There are 265 distinct `fmt` sources.** Systems 15, 35 and 55 are built from
   `moog900/modules.js`.
5. **Only Systems 15, 35 and 55 use the Moog modular engine.** Model 15 and Grandmother use
   the generic voice, `dsp-core`.

**`check.mjs` is the only test suite today.** It renders every sound through the DSP and fails
on silence, NaN, unknown controls or bad cables. Running `check.mjs tb-303` passes in about
35 s. Its numbers vary between runs, because oscillator phase and noise are random.

### The fork

empty: `components/app/`, `prisma/schema/app.prisma` (a stub), and `.context/app/`, which

## 2. The prototype is a source, never read by the app

**Ruling (D13): the live app never reads anything in `prototype/`.** Every prototype file
becomes one of three things, decided per file in §3. After that, the prototype is reference
material only.

| What the file holds                                                                                                                                                                  | What it becomes                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              | How the live app reads it                                                                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Content**: sounds and their lessons, lineage, "Unusual on…" notes, "What is not modelled" text, the databank                                                                       | **Database tables.** A conversion script exports each file once to JSON seed data under `prisma/seeds/app-knowyoursynth/data/`, and the seed writes it into tables (§9). After the first seed the tables are the source of truth, and admins edit them (D2). | **Only from the database**, through `/api/v1`. The seed data files are imported by the seed and nothing else. |
| **The instrument**: synth definitions (panel geometry, controls, jacks, areas, init, `toEngine`, `fmt` and jack functions), the DeepMind effects table, the Moog 900 module builders | **Typed TypeScript modules** in `lib/app/synths/defs/` (D1). This is app code, compiled into the build, and the same kind of thing as the engine. It is not read from `prototype/`.                                                                          | As compiled code, by id, from the definition registry.                                                        |
| **Program code**: engine, library, panel and UI                                                                                                                                      | **Typed modules and React components** in `lib/app/synths/` and `components/app/` (D3, transliterated).                                                                                                                                                      | As compiled code.                                                                                             |

Three guard tests make the ruling stick:

1. **Nothing outside `prototype/` imports from it** (`e1`).
2. **Nothing but the seed imports the seed data files** (`m2`).
3. **No content constant is left in a definition module** (`m1`). Presets, lineage, unusual
   notes and limits are refused there.

**Converting is a copy, not a link.** The conversion script, `prototype/tools/export-content.mjs`,
runs once per synth as part of that synth's port task. Its output is committed, and it is
never run by the app or the seed. `prototype/` is removed from the repo once every synth has
been ported (`x5`). The committed baseline and seed data mean nothing needs it after that.

## 4. What we are building

### In one paragraph

Anyone can open a synth, play it, patch it, take its lessons and read the databank without an
account. A signed-in user can also:

- save their own sounds and pick up where they left off;
- keep lesson progress and favourites;
- list the synths they own and want, and link YouTube, Spotify, Instagram and Bandcamp;
- publish sounds that others can play and copy, with credit.

A tutor answers questions about the synth in front of you, sound design, synthesis and the
people who made the music. It can also design or change a sound. You apply its change with
one click and can undo it. Every synth shows a neutral design unless its maker has approved
the faithful one.

### Two designs per synth (D11)

- **Faithful** is the prototype's design: the real panel's colours, lettering and maker marks.
- **Neutral** has the same layout, controls and jacks, so every sound and lesson works on
  both. It uses neutral colours and fonts and carries no logos or wordmarks. The app's text
  still names the synth.

The design is set per synth in admin. **Neutral is the default.** Faithful needs a recorded
vendor approval.

## 5. Ground rules

These apply to every feature. They combine Sunrise's rules, the `sunrise-fork` process tier,
and this plan's decisions.

- **The prototype is never read by the app** (D13, §2). Its content becomes tables. Its
  instrument and program code become typed modules.
- **Extend through seams** (`sunrise.golden-rule`).
  - New code goes in `lib/app/**`, `components/app/**`, `prisma/schema/app.prisma`,
    `prisma/seeds/app-knowyoursynth/`, new routes under `app/`, and `.context/app/`.
  - The documented exceptions are the marketing thin-shims, `dashboard/page.tsx`,
    `privacy` and `terms`, `sitemap.ts` and `robots.ts`.
  - Any other edit to a platform file gets a row in `.context/app/divergences.md` in the same
    PR, with a deletion trigger (`sunrise.divergences`). It also gets an issue filed with
    Sunrise, as a completing act of the task (`B7`).
- **The instrument is code; what is played on it and said about it is data** (D1). Nothing
  under `lib/app/synths/` imports a content table. A function there takes the content it needs
  as an argument.
- **A sound stands on its own.** Every stored sound records the definition version it was made
  on. It is validated by `lib/app/synths/validate.ts` against that definition, both on write
  and on read. A renamed control or jack id ships with a mapping from the old id.
- **The check gates the instrument** (D3). A ported synth merges only when it matches the
  prototype's baseline. When the engine changes, every synth ported so far must still match.
- **The validator gates generated content.** Anything the tutor proposes goes through the same
  validator as a user's save before anyone sees it.
- **API first.** Everything is an `/api/v1` endpoint before it is a button.
- **Every new table** gets:
  - an `onDelete` policy: `Cascade` for personal data, `SetNull` for retained content;
  - a declaration in `lib/app/data-export.ts`: a source if it holds data about a person,
    otherwise `excluded` with a reason (`export-sources.test.ts` fails on a model with neither);
  - a tenancy classification. A table every org shares goes on `GLOBAL_CONFIG_MODELS` (the
    divergence row for `lib/tenancy/classification.ts`).

  A drift probe in `lib/app/db-drift.ts` is only for an object Prisma cannot model: a
  hand-written foreign key, a partial or vector index, a check constraint. A table Prisma models
  in full needs none, because `db:drift-check` and the migration diff already see it.

  Erasure goes through `eraseUser()`.

- **Seeds are idempotent and safe on empty** (`fp4`). A row an admin has edited is
  operator-owned, and the seed never overwrites it.
- **Tests run on the harness this stack has** (`B9`). Unit tests mock the database. The synth
  check is a script: `npm run check:synths [id]`. Wiring is proved by a smoke run against the
  dev database.
- **Docs travel with code.** Each feature adds or updates a page under `.context/app/`:
  `synths.md`, `synth-port.md`, `catalogue.md`, `bank.md`, `sounds.md`, `sharing.md`,
  `tutor.md`, `rights.md`.
- **The gates** run before every PR, in this order: `/pre-pr`, `/security-review`,
  `/code-review`, `npm run format` (`flow.gates`). There are at most three review rounds.
- **Migrations** on tables with vector or partial indexes are created with `--create-only`.
  Their SQL is reviewed for spurious drops before it is applied (`B13`).

waits on it.

### How to read a block

Each field is named for the Hub argument it becomes (§7):

- **A phase** has a key, a `name`, a `summary` (one plain-text line) and a `description`.
- **A feature** has:
  - a `slug`, a `title` and a `summary` (one plain line);
  - `dependsOn`, as slugs;
  - a `description`: the situation first, then the **source files** it ports and what each
    becomes (`fp8`);
  - a `doneWhen` and `references`.
- **Indicative tasks** each have a ref, title, size, task dependencies, done-when and files
  in scope. These become `plan_feature`'s arguments when the feature is claimed. The claimer
  reconciles them against the tree first (`fp1`).

**What a done-when may contain.** A task's done-when holds only what can be proved at merge
(`fp3b`). Every task's done-when also includes **the gates, passed before the PR opens**. That
is written once here rather than in every row. Anything that needs a deployed build goes in
the feature's done-when instead.

## 8. The tutor — design

**Shape.** BeatBuddy's pattern (BeatBreaker §6):

- a tutor panel on the synth page, where the prototype's tutor sat;
- an app-owned stream route that pins the agent and passes the synth id and the current panel
  as `entityContext`;
- tools that execute server-side.

The model never writes to the panel. It proposes, the server validates, and the user applies.

**Tools.**

| Tool                   | What it does                                                                                                        |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `get_panel`            | Reads the panel the client sent: values, cables, definition version.                                                |
| `describe_synth`       | The synth's controls, jacks, areas and ranges, from the definition. It becomes a lookup if `t1` finds it too large. |
| `propose_panel_change` | Returns a change set: controls to set, and cables to add or remove. Validated.                                      |
| `design_sound`         | Returns a whole panel and a name, validated. The user can save it as one of their sounds, marked as from the tutor. |
| `search_knowledge`     | Sunrise's vector search over the databank (`k1`).                                                                   |
| `web_search`           | A search service when configured (`k2`). Otherwise the provider's own search, when it has one.                      |

**Sources.**

- Answers about a synth come from its definition and its notes.
- Answers about people and records try the databank first, then search, then the model's own
  knowledge.

The tutor always says which source it used.

**Guardrails and cost.**

- A daily allowance per user. The number is set at `x4` and recorded against D6.
- Per-turn caps.
- Topic boundaries.
- No tool takes a user id from its arguments.

**Provider.** The tutor shares BeatBuddy's provider and model choice. Its seed sets no
provider.

## 10. Decisions

### Decided — 2026-10-04

**D1 · Data or code** (brief Q1)

**The instrument is code; what is played on it and said about it is data.**

- **In code:** typed per-synth TypeScript modules: panel, controls, jacks, areas, init,
  `toEngine`, and the `fmt` and jack functions. `CONTRACT.md` becomes the `SynthDef` and
  `EngineParams` types. Control and jack ids are structural, like BeatBreaker's meters and
  lanes.
- **As rows:** the catalogue (listed, order, definition version), sounds with their lessons,
  lineage, notes and the databank.
- **Versions:** sounds are validated against a versioned definition, and saved sounds record
  the version. A renamed id ships with a mapping.

**D2 · Who adds synths** (Q2)

**Adding a synth is always developer work.** Admins list and order synths and edit sounds,
lessons, lineage, notes and the databank without a deploy, with an audit log. Users make
their own sounds, not synths.

**D3 · Porting order** (Q8)

**Transliterate, don't rewrite.**

1. Make the check deterministic and record a baseline from the prototype.
2. Port the engine, the shared library and the renderer.
3. Port Model D end to end as the pattern, then the rest, with the check gating each one.
4. Sounds become seed data.

**D4 · What a user owns** (Q3)

- **Sounds.** A user's own sounds: panel, cables, definition version, name and notes. Saving
  a library sound makes a credited copy. A sound the tutor designs is one of yours, marked as
  from the tutor.
- **On the account:** lesson progress, favourites, preferences, and the last panel per synth.
- **Synths and links.** The synths you own and a wishlist, picked from an exhaustive databank,
  with Suggest a synth for missing ones. Links to YouTube, Spotify, Instagram and Bandcamp.
- **Profile.** A public profile page, public or private as the user sets it.
- **Elsewhere.** Screen-only settings stay in the browser. Tutor history lives in Sunrise's
  conversation storage.

**D5 · Sharing** (Q4)

**BeatBreaker's model, without variations or sessions.**

- A sound is private, shared by link, or published, and publishing needs a username.
- There is a public sound page that plays signed out.
- Copies carry credit, and there is reporting and unlisting.
- Sharing comes after My sounds and before the tutor.

**D6 · The tutor** (Q6)

**BeatBuddy's pattern.**

- A seeded agent with no provider in its seed, sharing BeatBuddy's provider and model choice.
- Server-side tools return validated proposals, which the user applies with Undo.
- A daily allowance per user.
- A spike on the DeepMind's prompt size, with a lookup tool replacing the description if the
  prompt is too big.
- It **also answers questions** about sound design, artists and how synths work.

**D7 · Retrieval**

**Use Sunrise's vector store for RAG where it fits.** The databank is the first corpus.

**D8 · The BeatBreaker link** (Q5)

**Separate accounts.**

- The two apps look like one family and link to each other.
- A shared tempo and MIDI clock come later.
- One domain or two is the owner's call. Nothing in the build depends on it.

**D9 · The databank** (Q7)

**It lives in this app as proper tables.**

- Admins edit it, users suggest synths, and it is loaded into the vector store for the tutor.
- BeatBreaker links to its pages.
- It grows into the reference list of every synth, compiled from facts.

**D10 · Mobile** (Q9)

**Playing a full panel needs a landscape tablet or a desktop.** Phones get everything else,
with a zoomable panel and a rotate prompt. Everything is in the API, so native apps can follow.

**D11 · Rights** (Q10)

**Two designs per synth, neutral by default.**

- **The designs.** The faithful designs are kept. Neutral changes the look only: the same
  layout, controls and jacks, neutral colours and fonts, and no logos or wordmarks. The app's
  text still names the synth.
- **Switching.** Set per synth in admin. Faithful only with a recorded vendor approval.
- **Also:**
  - paraphrase the manuals, never quote them;
  - a not-affiliated notice;
  - takedowns through report and unlist;
  - a legal review before launch.

**D12 · Tutor web search**

**Both routes.**

- A search service through `call-external-api`, counted in the allowance and off without a
  key.
- The provider's own web search, where the model has one.

An admin setting chooses between them.

**D13 · The prototype at runtime** (owner, 2026-10-04)

**The live app never reads the prototype's files.**

- **Content becomes tables.** Sounds, lessons, lineage, notes, limits and the databank are
  exported once to seed data and seeded into tables, which are then the source of truth. The
  app reads them only through the API.
- **The instrument and the program become typed code** in the app. Nothing outside
  `prototype/` imports from it, and nothing but the seed imports the seed data. Guard tests
  enforce both.
- **Removal.** `prototype/` is removed once every synth has been ported (`x5`).

### Still open

2. **Behringer recreations in the reference list.** Decided by the owner when `f-databank` is

3. **The allowance number, the provider and the model.** Decided at `x4`.
4. **CHANGELOG.** This fork does not publish releases. The proposal is no entries, unless the

## 11. Risks

| Risk                                                                      | Mitigation                                                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **The AudioWorklet or the sound-map worker does not load under Next 16.** | `e3` requires both in dev and in a production build. The ScriptProcessor and main-thread fallbacks are kept.                                     |
| **A port changes the sound subtly.**                                      | The deterministic baseline (`e1`), compared for every sound of every ported synth, and re-run on every earlier synth when the engine changes.    |
| **Content is lost or altered in conversion.**                             | Every export task compares row counts with the prototype. Every seeded sound passes the validator, and the ported check plays the seeded sounds. |
| **The DeepMind is too big**: 1,568 controls, 1.3 MB.                      | `p3` measures its first render. `t1` measures its prompt size.                                                                                   |
| **The seed overwrites an admin's edit.**                                  | Admin-edited rows are operator-owned, and the seed skips them (`c1`, `fp4`).                                                                     |
| **The tutor states false facts about real people.**                       | Databank first, citations, source labelling, topic boundary, and an eval set (`k3`).                                                             |
| **Likeness and trade dress.**                                             | Neutral by default, faithful only with an approval, and a legal review (`x2`).                                                                   |
| **Profiles and links are used for spam.**                                 | Links limited to the four platforms' hosts. Private by default. Reporting and moderation.                                                        |
