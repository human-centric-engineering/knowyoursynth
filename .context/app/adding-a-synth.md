# Adding a new synth

The recipe for a synth with no prototype version, written straight into the app (`f-donner-b1`).
The prototype is removed at launch (D13), so every synth after the first 25 comes this way. It
is [`synth-port.md`](./synth-port.md) with the prototype taken out: the same six steps, but the
definition and the content are written by hand, and the check baseline and goldens are recorded
once from that definition instead of reproduced from the prototype.

The worked example is the Donner B1 (`donner-b1`): its definition, its sounds, lineage and notes,
its baseline and its goldens.

## 1. Definition

Write `lib/app/synths/defs/<id>.ts`, typed `SynthDef`, starting from the nearest definition
already in the app (the B1 started from `defs/td-3.ts`). Read the synth's folder under
[`synth-data/`](./synth-data/README.md) first: the spec, and the manual and photo in its `raw/`. Measure
positions on the photo, and put its path and size in the docblock. Register it in `SYNTH_DEFS` (`defs/index.ts`)
and `LOADERS` (`defs/load.ts`); `registry.test.ts` fails if one is missing.

- `version: 1`, and no content: no presets, lineage, unusual notes or limits
  (`registry.test.ts` refuses them).
- `toEngine()` maps onto the existing `EngineParams` only. If the voice needs something the
  engine does not have, that is engine work, planned on its own, not part of adding the synth.
- Controls the app cannot model yet (a sequencer's buttons, MIDI settings) are still drawn, and
  their help says what they do on the hardware and what they do here (the TB-303 precedent).

Add the id to `NATIVE_SYNTHS` in `scripts/native-synths.ts`. That explicit list is how the
scripts and tests tell a native synth from a ported one; a typo in a registry is otherwise
refused as an orphan, not taken for a new synth. Then record its goldens, once:

```bash
npx tsx scripts/record-synth-goldens.ts <id>
```

The recorder takes a native synth's goldens from its own definition and refuses to overwrite
them. `goldens.test.ts` then holds the definition to them. Write
`tests/unit/lib/app/synths/defs/<id>.test.ts` for the engine params at init and for each switch
(the B1's mirrors `model-d.test.ts`).

### Routing

Write `lib/app/synths/routing/<id>.ts` from the spec's `modules` and `connections`, in the definition's control and
jack ids, and add it to `SYNTH_ROUTINGS` (`routing/index.ts`). Start from `routing/model-d.ts`; the rules are in
[`synths.md`](./synths.md#signal-routing). `routing.test.ts` fails until every id it names exists in the definition,
every control the sound hears is in some block's `controls`, and every spec module and connection is drawn or
`omitted` with a reason.

## 2. Brand tags

As for a port: every maker's mark in `decor` gets `brand: true` (the B1's DONNER logo and the
printed "DONNER SYN BASS / ANALOG SYNTHESIZER AND SEQUENCER").

## 3. Content, by hand

There is no export. Work from the owner's spec for the synth
(`.context/app/synth-data/<id>/<id>.synth.json`; its manual and photo are in that folder's
local-only `raw/`): its parameters, ports,
behaviours, `not_in_source` and `modelling_gaps` are what the notes and limits say, and nothing
in them may contradict it. Where the spec says a fact is missing, say so rather than fill it in. Write the files the export would have written, under
`prisma/seeds/app-knowyoursynth/data/`:

- `sounds/<id>.json`: `{ synth, version, sounds }`, in the format of any exported file. Every
  sound is a lesson: its `steps` build it from the panel's defaults. Riffs are
  `[beat, midi, lengthBeats, velocity?]`; velocity 1 is an accent (the acid threshold is 0.9), a
  note longer than its step slides into the next, and repeated short notes inside a step are a
  ratchet. The B1's were generated by a short script and committed as JSON.
- `lineage/<id>.json`: what it descends from and its relatives.
- `notes/<id>.json`: unusual notes (each `target` a control, jack or area of the definition)
  and the limits, with a "What is not modelled" entry for whatever the panel draws but the app
  does not do.
- A row in `synths.json`, at its place in the picker.
- A databank instrument in `data/bank/instruments.json`, with `app: ["<id>"]` and a `source`.
  `recreationOf` only if it is a recreation of another instrument; the B1 is inspired by the
  TB-303, so it has none. If its maker has no other entry, add the maker's other synths to
  `reference.json` (`reference.test.ts` checks every maker is covered).

Extend `tests/unit/lib/app/catalogue/seed-data.test.ts` to the synth's counts and its place in
the listing.

## 4. Seed

```bash
npm run db:seed
```

The same seed and the same validator as a port. A refusal is a mistake in the hand-written
content or the definition. Never loosen the validator.

## 5. First baseline

```bash
npm run check:synths -- <id> --write-baseline
npm run check:synths -- <id> --compare
```

The first records `.context/app/check/<id>.json` from the definition and the seed sounds; it
writes one native synth at a time, never a ported one, and never over an existing file. Read
its table before committing: no sound should clip (peak under 1) or be silent. From then on the
synth is held to its baseline exactly as a ported one is ([`check/README.md`](./check/README.md)).

## 6. Page

As for a port: once listed, `/synths/<id>` plays it, and `synth-page-catalogue.test.tsx` opens
it. Look at it as [`synth-port.md`](./synth-port.md#6-page) says.
